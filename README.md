# Be The Puck

**Pick a thinker. Pick a stock. See the next move.**

Be The Puck has two parts:

1. **The plays site** (home, `/strategies/` = Plays, `/thinkers/` = Analysts,
   `/stocks/`, `/learn/`, `/method/`): 100 published trading plays tied to the 84
   analyst records behind them (bios and rules checked against their books, papers and
   interviews), run on about 380 stocks, ETFs, indexes and coins since 2005,
   every call graded, and the close that flips each play next.
2. **The live desk** (`/desk/`): real-time news scored for revenue impact
   (documented below).

## Plays site

Generated by Python at build time. Netlify runs the build on every push and
publishes `_site/` (see `netlify.toml`); generated pages are not committed.

`/articles/sectors/<group>/`) · `/strategies/<play>/` · `/thinkers/<analyst>/` ·

**Method.** Signals use finished daily, weekly (Fri; Sun for crypto) or
month-end bars and are acted on at the next session; 5 bps per switch (10 for
crypto, 30 for micro caps); T-bill yield (^IRX) while out; long or cash only.
The next move is found numerically: the engine asks each play what it would say
after one more bar at a grid of hypothetical closes and bisects every flip point.
Calendar plays report the date of their next change.

**Refresh.** `.github/workflows/refresh-prices.yml` runs after each U.S. close:
it adds reader-requested tickers, fetches prices, macro series and fundamentals,
and commits `data/`; that push triggers the Netlify build.

**Pick your plays.** Readers can count only the plays they trust: a ready-made set (the
most accurate play in each family, the top 3 in each family, plays right more than half the
time) or their own pick. `assets/plays.js` keeps the choice in the browser and, signed in, on
the account (`POST /api/account/plays`); `?plays=top1|top3|half|all|<slug.slug>` shares a view.
The home screener (with a "Most accurate plays agree" sort), the stock list (Your signal, Plays in,
the family board), each stock page (Plays in, the score, the most accurate play in each family,
the full list with each play's calls right on that stock), the watchlist and the account page all
recount from `data/playset.json`. "Accurate" is the share of a play's finished calls that went
the right way on the real stocks (no funds, indexes or coins) since 2005; a play needs 500+
calls on at least half the stocks to be ranked (`render.rankable`). Top 10 and Top picks always
use every play.

**Your signal.** Readers weight 18 factors (`tools/qstrat/factors.py`): plays in, analysts'
upside and rating, P/E, forward P/E, PEG, price to sales and book, dividend yield, earnings and
revenue growth, profit margin, return on equity, debt to equity, off the high, trend, 1-year
momentum and crash exposure. Each scores a stock 0-100 (company numbers by rank among the covered stocks); the score
is the weighted average over the factors the stock has, and the reader's Buy and Sell lines (60/40
to start) make it Buy, Hold or Sell on the stock page (with a factor-by-factor breakdown), the
stock list, the screener, the watchlist and the account page. The standard weights (`DEFAULT`: plays 40, off the high 30, trend 30, since October 13, 2026) use
only factors with a tested record (`factorgrade.py`: +6.0 points of 12-month edge, against +4.4 for
the old plays 50 / analysts' upside 50). **Off the high** is the exit rule: 100 points at the 1-year
high, none at 30% or more below. Analysts' upside is capped at 50 points while the price is under
its 200-day average, so a falling stock can't score better because its target now looks further
away. **Model version 3** (October 21, 2026, `MODEL = 3`) adds two rules on top of the weights, both
graded since 2006 by `modelcompare.py` first: the **market guard** (`GUARD`): while a stock's bench
index is 15% or more below its own 1-year high, the price factors (`PRICE_KEYS`: off the high,
trend, 1-year momentum) carry no weight and the rest of the weights decide, for every set of
weights; and **sticky calls** (`MID`): on the standard setup only (standard weights and lines, every
play counted), a Buy holds until the score drops below 50 and a Sell until it rises above 50. The
state comes from `callpath.py`, the standard signal's day-by-day path rebuilt point-in-time each
build from 2025-01-01 (nothing is stored); `playset.json` carries the guard (`gd`, `gb`), the sticky
call with the score it goes with (`st`) and the keys, and plays.js / signal.mjs apply them only when
their own score is the same number. The stock page says when either rule is deciding ("Held at
Buy…", "Market guard on…", price factors marked "stands down"), the flip lines use the sticky
thresholds, and the alert emails say "held at Buy" (reads.json `hd`, `gd`). `factors.MODEL` is bumped whenever the standard weights or a factor's scoring change: saved
weights that were just the old standard move to the new one (plays.js, signal.mjs), and the alert
emails re-baseline quietly instead of mailing every flip the change caused. Saved like the plays;
`?w=plays50.pe20&buy=60&sell=40`. The watchlist and the account page note which signals changed since the
reader last looked (per browser, `BPPlays.changes`), and the watchlist flags one name being 25% or
more of the list by value.

**Old vs new, tested.** `tools/model_compare.py` (from the dev engine cache; a few minutes) runs
`tools/qstrat/modelcompare.py` and writes `data/modelcompare.json`, which the free article
`/articles/old-vs-new-signal/` reads: every month-end since 2006, the new standard, the plays alone and
a dozen candidate refinements scored point-in-time, with how often Buys and Sells beat the market,
how often they fell 30%+ within a year, and how often calls flip; plus the old standard rebuilt from
the price targets in `data/events/` (July 2024 on). Rerun it whenever the model changes.

**The price that would change the signal's mind.** `factors.flip_lines` (and `BPPlays.flips` in the
browser, the same arithmetic) rescans prices from 40% below to 80% above the close, moving off the
high, trend, momentum and analysts' upside with the price and holding the plays and company numbers
where they are, and reports the nearest close below and above at which the call changes. Shown on
the stock page's signal card (redrawn for the reader's weights), under the watchlist's signal pill,
and as the last line of each name's read in reads.json (so the alert emails carry it). The inputs
(`pi`, `rk` in playset.json) are rounded to 4 decimals on both sides so the two agree.

**This week's calls, graded** (`/articles/calls-graded/`, `tools/qstrat/scorecard.py`, free, nightly):
every session's standard-weight calls since October 13, 2026 are rebuilt point-in-time from prices
and the plays' call histories (nothing stored, so nothing can be tidied up), then graded: the latest
week's Buys, Holds and Sells (median, share that rose, share that beat their market, best and worst
names), the running week-by-week record, and daily equal-weight baskets against SPY.

**Planning an account.** `/guides/plan-an-account/` (`tools/qstrat/accountplan.py`) reads
`data/accountplan.json`, written by `tools/account_study.py` from the dev cache: 2,000 random
portfolios per setting from the covered stocks with full history since 2006 (1 to 30 stocks, a core
with a slice of picks, one big position capped or not). The "core" there is all of those stocks in
equal amounts, so survivors are compared with survivors; the page says why SPY isn't. The build
writes `data/px/<slug>.json` (weekly closes since 2006) for the watchlist's **account check**
(`assets/acctcheck.js`): the reader's own mix at their own amounts (in Canadian dollars, using
`usdcad` in watch.json), its worst fall, worst week and crash results, next to the same mix with a
cap on any one name and an index core (SPY or XIU). It all runs in the browser. The watchlist's
file import reads holdings exports from Wealthsimple ("Holdings report (CSV)"), Questrade, IBKR, TD,
RBC, Schwab, Fidelity, Vanguard and E*TRADE by their column names.

**Disclaimer on every page.** The footer of every page carries `render.DISCLAIMER`; Netlify's
not-found page is the site's own `404.html` (`Site.not_found_html`). The build stops if any page
lacks the disclaimer (`build_strategies.pages_without_disclaimer`) and `tools/smoke.py` checks it on
every page it opens. The footer links to **Ways to invest, by country** (`/guides/ways-to-invest/`,
`tools/qstrat/waystoinvest.py`): for Canada (with Quebec), the U.S., the U.K. and Australia, the kinds
of help, the duty each owes by law, costs, official registers, SPIVA results, investor protection,
tax-sheltered accounts, red flags and some firms' own published prices (alphabetical, never ranked).
Every fact links its source; `CHECKED` is the date they were last checked. Recheck the account limits
each January and the SPIVA figures when new scorecards come out (March and September).

**News that moves stocks.** `tools/fetch_events.py` (nightly) keeps each company's earnings
dates with EPS surprises and its analyst rating changes; `tools/qstrat/newsevents.py` sorts them,
the 8-K filings and the headlines into kinds, measures each reaction against the stock's index,
and grades each kind by how reliably it moved stocks the expected way. Stock pages show what moved
the stock lately and its next earnings date; `/guides/news-that-moves-stocks/` has the table.

**Bring your own stocks.** The watchlist keeps each reader's list in their
browser (and, signed in, in their account: `assets/mine.js`). Tickers Be The Puck doesn't cover are queued
(`netlify/lib/watchq.mjs`, symbols only) from the watchlist (`/api/watch`) or the account page (`/api/account/follow`
keeps them on the account as `asked` and moves each into the followed list once it's covered). The nightly job
validates up to 25 a night (300 in total) and the build runs all plays on them. **Reader-requested names are
private:** their pages are built but noindex and out of the sitemap, and they stay out of the stock list, the
screener, the board, the scoreboards, names.json and the account page's Find (reads.json marks them `pv`). They are
ranked against the public names without joining them (`factors._rank`, `callpath.daily_path`), so adding one never
moves a public stock's score. `PRIVATE=SYM python tools/dev_render.py render …` treats covered names as private to test it.

**Learn + glossary.** Terms live in `tools/qstrat/glossary.py`;
`tools/qstrat/linker.py` links the first use of each term on every generated page
and in the output copies of `stories/*.html` and `desk/index.html`.
`assets/glossary.js` shows the hover/tap definitions and links terms inside the
live desk's feed from `data/glossary.json`.

**Universe.** Market indexes, U.S. sector funds and the stock groups in
`content.py`, all 100 Nasdaq-100 companies (`NDX_NEW` / `NDX_COVERED`; the ones
added in bulk appear in every list), the 218 Toronto-listed companies on Proud to Work's TSX tracker
(`TSX_NEW` / `TSX_COVERED`, prices in C$, a page for every play; the TSX filter
on `/stocks/`), plus reader requests. Names added in bulk (`bulk`) stay off the
home page's board. Micro caps carry a 0.30% switching cost.

**Open item.** The PB EMA's real lengths are unconfirmed (its TradingView page
was taken down 30 Sep 2026). It is tested with 12/21 EMAs; change the lengths in `plays_fn.pb_ema`
and drop its `flag` in `plays.py` once confirmed.

**Members.** `/picks/` (the top-picks tracker), `/rules/` (their own Start/Stop
rules) and every report under `/articles/` (not the hub) are for members, except the free
"Risk and selling" pieces (`tools/qstrat/riskpieces.py`, listed in its `FREE` and in
`member-gate.js` `excludedPath`). `netlify/edge-functions/member-gate.js`
serves the full page only with a signed member cookie; otherwise it serves the
page's locked version (title, opening line, sign-in / join card) that
`tools/qstrat/members.py` builds under `/locked/`. `netlify/functions/member.mjs`
signs members in (password checked against `QM_PASS_HASH`, a PBKDF2 hash; the
password itself is never stored) and keeps each member's saved list.
`netlify/functions/paypal.mjs` handles the $5/month PayPal subscription once
`PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID`, `PAYPAL_CLIENT_SECRET`, `PAYPAL_PLAN_ID` and
`PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID` are set (`PAYPAL_ENV=sandbox` for testing); emails go
through Resend with `RESEND_API_KEY`. Cookies are signed with `QM_SECRET`.
To change the members' password, set a new `QM_PASS_HASH` (everyone signed in
with the old one is signed out):
`python -c "import hashlib,os,base64;b=lambda x:base64.urlsafe_b64encode(x).rstrip(b'=').decode();s=os.urandom(16);print('pbkdf2-sha256\$310000\$'+b(s)+'\$'+b(hashlib.pbkdf2_hmac('sha256',input('password: ').encode(),s,310000)))"`

**Top picks.** `tools/qstrat/picks.py`: five picks from the strongest setups,
reviewed after each Friday close, held while in the top 10. The history lives in
Netlify Blobs behind `/api/picks-state` (token `PICKS_STATE_TOKEN`), not in this
repo; only the production build writes it. Locally, set `QUIPLEE_PICKS_FILE` to a
JSON file.

**Home page and screener.** The home page answers two questions in four numbered
steps. Step 1, "Is the market about to crash?", is the nine crash gauges' verdict
(`macro.py`) and the plays on the S&P 500. Steps 2 to 4 are a screener of every
covered name (`screen.py`, `assets/screen.js`, data in `data/screen.json`): buying
or selling (a play agrees with buying when it holds the stock, with selling when
it's out), where to look (market, sector, crash exposure, a name), and how many of
the plays must agree. The first 25 rows are rendered at build time; the filters,
sorting, ready-made screens and the ☆ follow run in the browser and sync to the URL
(`?side=sell&mkt=ca&min=75`). The filter rules in `screen.py` and `screen.js` must match.

**Theory tester.** `/theory/` (`theory.py`, `assets/theory.js`): pick a stock and a
theory (one play, a family of plays or all of them) and get the reading (buy, don't
buy or split), the rationale (the rule that applies, tonight's indicator readings,
the checks, since when, the price that would change its mind, the record on that
stock), then step or auto-roll through every play's reason to buy or not, best
record first. Each covered name's data is `data/theory/<slug>.json`, written by the
pair-page workers (`_pairs_for` in `render.py`) while that stock's chart windows are
loaded, so it adds seconds to the build, not minutes. The page renders NVDA with
all 100 plays by default; `?s=<slug>&t=all|fam:<family>|play:<slug>` picks another.
Paper trading, which this replaced in October 2026, is gone from the site
(`/paper/*` redirects here); its old portfolios are left untouched in the Blobs
store `paper`.

**One verdict: Your signal.** Since October 2026 the site makes one call per stock, Your
signal (Buy, Hold, Sell from the reader's weights; see above). It heads every stock page and
is the first column on the stock list, the screener, the watchlist and the account page;
`data/reads.json` carries each stock's standard signal (`S`, `sc`) and read. The old Start/Stop
light (`Site.light`: on at 60% of plays in, off at 40%) is no longer shown; only the Top 10's
published rule and the members' `/rules/` page still use it.

**Stock x play pages.** Only each stock's headline plays (the most accurate in each family,
plus the Mayer Multiple) and every play on SPY (the Learn examples) get a page; any other
`/stocks/<stock>/<play>/` forwards to that play's row on the stock page (`netlify.toml`). That
cut a deploy from about 40,000 files to about 6,000. Link with `site.pair_link`.

**One account (Login in the menu, `/me/`).** A Be The Puck account (email or username plus a
password) is shared by the account page (`/me/`; the menu says Login when signed out and Account when signed in), the stocks a person follows,
their emails and the member tools. Accounts live in the Blobs store `paper`
(`u/<name>`, with `e/<hash>` mapping an email to its account); the logic is in
`netlify/lib/account.mjs` and the routes in `netlify/functions/account.mjs`
(`/api/account/*`: sign-up, sign-in, follow, alerts, email, confirm, forgot/reset
password, unsubscribe, delete). Old username-only accounts from the retired game keep working and
can add an email on the account page. Its "My stocks" table (`assets/me.js` tableHtml) shows your signal, plays in, price, 1-year change and any
of the 17 factors as columns (Columns picker with quick picks; values from data/playset.json, green at 60+ points, red at
40 or less), filters by signal, group, name and "favours buying"/"counts against" on any factor, sorts by any header
(sortable.js; the phone card view gets its Sort by menu) and keeps the view per browser (`bp.me.view.v1`).

**The classics (`/classics/`, `tools/qstrat/classics.py` + `classics.json`).** Fifteen strategies people stopped
talking about (Dow Theory, Livermore's Market Key, Gann, Gould's three steps and a stumble, Zweig, Granville, Darvas,
Donchian and the Turtles, Coppock, Graham, Dogs of the Dow, the Hindenburg Omen, the odd-lot theory, Arms, Dysart),
researched and sourced Oct 27, 2026: the story, the original rule, whether it can be tested on the site's data (and

not yet on the site (Livermore's Market Key, Graham's defensive test, the Positive Volume Index, Gann's swing trend,

**Articles, signed in (`assets/artmine.js`).** The hub leads with "About your stocks": a brief per stock in

**Emails (Resend).** All mail goes through Resend (`netlify/lib/mail.mjs`,
`freealerts.mjs`, `accountmail.mjs`). To switch it on: make a Resend account,
add the domain `bethepuck.com` there and copy the DNS records it gives you
(a DKIM TXT record, an SPF TXT and an MX record on the `send` subdomain) into
Netlify DNS for bethepuck.com; once Resend shows the domain verified, create an
API key with sending access and set `RESEND_API_KEY` in Netlify (Site configuration →
Environment variables; a changed variable reaches the functions on the next deploy, so push
or redeploy after setting it). Optional: `QM_FROM` (default `Be The Puck <alerts@bethepuck.com>`),
`QM_REPLY_TO` (an address someone reads, so replies don't bounce off alerts@), `QM_OWNER_EMAIL`
(gets a note for each new account, and the password member's alerts)
and `QM_MAIL_ADDRESS` (the postal address Canada's anti-spam law asks for, shown in
every email footer). Until the key is set, sign-ups work and confirm links queue;
`alerts-nightly.mjs` (hourly) sends them on its first run after the key is in, then
sends each account one "your stocks tonight" email per close when something changed:
flips of the account's own signal (free; `netlify/lib/signal.mjs` scores it with the
account's saved weights and plays) and, for members, every play that got in or out and prices
near a play's line. Resend's free tier is 3,000 emails a month and 100 a day; past
that, its Pro plan.

**Members' own rules.** `/rules/` (`tools/qstrat/rules.py`, `assets/rules.js`)
lets a member choose which plays count toward the light (all, "Best record" and
"Crash protection" from the guides' nightly rankings, a family preset, or their
own pick), move the 60/40 thresholds, and check their watchlist against four
limits: biggest stock, biggest sector or group, share in names that are Start
under their rule, and share in High / Very high crash exposure. The light is
walked through `data/hist/<slug>.json` (two years of weekly calls per stock)
exactly as `Site.light` does, so "every play, 60/40" matches the site's light on
every name. The rule is saved with the member's list (`PUT /api/member/list
{rules}`); weights come from the quantities or values in the watchlist file.

**Free email alerts.** `/alerts/` signs people up (email + stocks, confirmed by

  custom weights; 30 calls a day per address), ending with a line on how to get the full data. Members make a personal key

## Live desk (`/desk/`)

**News breaks. We tell you what it's worth.**

A zero-build static web app: a real-time, news-feel desk that reads every market
story and tells you its **revenue impact** and **anticipated move** on the stock —
with **lookback** (how past stories moved that stock) and **similar-story**
evidence (how comparable events moved peers). Includes a **Story Screener**
(arm a topic, get pinged on revenue-impacting hits) and **smart filters** by
story type, impact, and direction.

See [BRAND.md](BRAND.md) for the full brand & content strategy.

Under each story, a **measure it** note gives the last close before the story and, once the closes are in, the next-day and five-day move against the S&P 500 (SPY) and the stock's normal daily swing, with a link to the story's dot on the stock's chart.

## Run locally


Open <http://localhost:5060> (strategy site) or <http://localhost:5060/desk/> (live desk). Sign in with any valid-looking email + any password
(client-side demo auth → local `q_session` cookie).

## How it works

```
desk/index.html login + app shell (feed / screener / alerts / watchlist) + detail modal
styles.css      newsroom × terminal theme (violet accent; green/red = impact only)
js/auth.js      cookie session gate (client-side demo)
js/data.js      universe, ~18 seed stories w/ outcomes, the IMPACT ENGINE,
                lookback() + similar() lookups, and the live story generator
js/store.js     watched tickers, armed topics, filters, alerts, live buffer
js/ui.js        all rendering: story cards, detail (impact/lookback/similar), toasts
js/app.js       boot, real-time loop, alert logic, event wiring
```

**The impact engine** (`js/data.js → evaluate()`): classifies each story by type +
headline keywords into a verdict — *revenue-impacting?*, direction, magnitude,
expected move range, confidence, and the revenue *mechanism*. Curated seed stories
ship with hand-tuned verdicts and realized outcomes; live-generated stories are
scored by the same `evaluate()` so the desk feels real in real time.

**Alerts** (the spec's core): when a live story is revenue-impacting **and** hits a
**watched ticker**, Be The Puck reports the stock; when it hits an **armed topic**, it
fires the alert about the affected stock. Both land in **Alerts** + a toast.

**Lookback & similar:** open any story → see how past stories moved that ticker and
how comparable events (same type / shared topic) moved peers (median 5-day move).

## Live news ingestion (real data)

A Netlify Function pulls **real headlines** and scores them with the same engine:

```
netlify/functions/news.js   server-side fetch of Google News RSS (per ticker +
                            per topic) and recent SEC EDGAR 8-K filings; returns
                            normalized stories. Zero npm deps; no browser CORS.
js/live.js                  fetches /.netlify/functions/news, enriches each item
                            via classifyType() -> topic map -> evaluate()
```

Flow: on load, `app.js` calls the function with your watched tickers + armed
topics. If it answers → **LIVE** mode (real news, polled every 60s; new
revenue-impacting hits fire alerts + optional browser notifications). If it's
unreachable (e.g. local `serve`, which doesn't run functions) → **DEMO** mode
(synthetic generator). The header pill shows which.

**Tagged-news APIs (optional, recommended):** the function also supports
Finnhub and Marketaux — set either env var in Netlify (Site settings →
Environment variables) and redeploy; no code change needed:

- `FINNHUB_KEY` — per-ticker company news, free tier 60 calls/min (finnhub.io)
- `MARKETAUX_KEY` — one multi-ticker call with entity tags, free ~100/day (marketaux.com)

Stories from all sources are deduped client-side by normalized headline.

**Local dev:** `npx serve` shows DEMO mode (no functions). To run the functions
locally, use `netlify dev` instead.

## Real outcomes & the scoreboard

Real stories persist in `localStorage` (`q_stories`, cap 200) and **age into
evidence**: `js/outcomes.js` joins each story's timestamp to the real daily
close series (from `netlify/functions/prices.js`, 6-month range) — baseline =
last close before publication, then realized **next-day** and **5-day** moves.

- **Story detail** shows *Anticipated* vs **Realized so far** (with a
  ✓ correct / ✗ missed direction grade).
- **Lookback / Similar** rank real outcomes (marked ✓) above the demo seed set.
- **Model scoreboard** (right rail) grades every real, revenue-impacting,
  directional call against the actual next-day move — calibration in public,
  per the brand's "honest by design" pillar.
