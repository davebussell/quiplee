The seven rules in plain English

Each rule on Quiplee in two or three sentences: who it comes from, how fast it is, and what it's good for.

The seven rules fall into three families. Band rules (PB EMA, Bull Market Support Band, Ripster cloud) watch the space between two moving averages. Checklist rules (Trend Template, Stage Analysis) add conditions, such as the average itself rising. Monthly rules (10-month SMA, 12-month momentum) look only once a month, which makes them slow but cheap to run.

daily · fast

PB EMA band

From Bitcoin Playboy

Two short exponential averages make a band. A daily close above the whole band means in; below it means out. Inside the band, it keeps its last call.

Good for: Quick reactions on volatile assets like crypto. On index funds it switches so often that costs and whipsaws eat most of the benefit.

weekly · medium

Bull Market Support Band

From Benjamin Cowen

Ben Cowen's band of a 20-week simple and a 21-week exponential average, checked every Friday. Above the band means a healthy bull market; below it means caution.

Good for: Bitcoin and broad indexes, where it has historically marked the big regime changes with few trades.

daily · fast

Ripster 34/50 EMA Cloud

From Ripster

Ripster's cloud between the 34- and 50-day exponential averages, used to decide whether to lean bullish or bearish.

Good for: Short-term traders who want a simple daily bias. Slower and steadier than the PB EMA.

daily · medium

Minervini Trend Template

From Mark Minervini

Mark Minervini's seven-point checklist: price above rising 50, 150 and 200-day averages, near its yearly high and well off its low. All seven must pass to get in.

Good for: Picking strong individual stocks. It's strict, so it spends a lot of time out, and Quiplee's exit rule (a close below the 50-day) is an assumption.

weekly · slow

Weinstein Stage Analysis

From Stan Weinstein

Stan Weinstein's rule: own a stock only when its weekly close is above a 30-week average that is itself rising.

Good for: Spotting the long 'Stage 2' advances in individual stocks and avoiding long declines.

month-end · slowest

Faber 10-month SMA

From Meb Faber

Meb Faber's rule: on the last day of each month, hold if the price is above its 10-month average; otherwise sit in T-bills.

Good for: Limiting crash damage on whole markets with only one or two trades a year.

month-end · slowest

Time-series momentum (12-month)

From Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen

From Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen: hold if the past 12 months' return beats cash; otherwise sit in T-bills. Checked monthly.

Good for: Broad markets and asset classes. Very few trades, but slow to get back in after a sharp recovery.

Plus one benchmark

Buy and hold, the approach John Bogle championed: own the market and never sell. It's the yardstick every rule above is measured against.
